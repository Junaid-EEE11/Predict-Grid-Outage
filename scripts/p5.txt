### Question 33 (Verbal / Power Systems Domain)
**Question:** Describe the IEEE 123-bus radial distribution feeder testbed. What are its nominal voltage levels, topology features, and aggregate active/reactive loads?
**Ideal Answer:**
- **Topology & Voltage:** The IEEE 123-bus feeder is a benchmark radial distribution system operating at a nominal $4.16$ kV line-to-line ($2.4$ kV line-to-neutral) voltage, fed from a $115\text{ kV} / 4.16\text{ kV}$ substation transformer at Bus 150.
- **Circuit Characteristics:** Features three-phase main trunk lines, two-phase and single-phase overhead laterals, unbalanced spot and distributed loads, and voltage regulators.
- **Aggregate Base Load:** Nominal total active power load is $P_{\text{base}} = 3,490$ kW ($1,742.1$ kW active load in benchmark configuration) and reactive power load $Q_{\text{base}} = 1,920$ kVAR, serving diverse commercial and residential load points.

### Question 34 (Written / Power-Flow Mathematics)
**Question:** How does `OpenDSSDirect.py` solve the non-linear multi-phase AC power-flow equations, and how do you computationally verify convergence and bus voltage feasibility?
**Ideal Answer:**
OpenDSS formulates the multi-phase network using the nodal admittance matrix $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{bus}}$ and solves the non-linear current injection equations using the Fixed-Point / Current-Injection Iteration method:
$$\mathbf{V}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{Y}_{\text{bus}}^{-1} \mathbf{I}_{\text{inj}}(\mathbf{V}^{(k)})$$
where current injection at node $m$, phase $\phi$ is $\mathbf{I}_{m, \phi} = \left( \frac{P_{m, \phi} + j Q_{m, \phi}}{\mathbf{V}_{m, \phi}} \right)^*$.
- **Verification of Convergence:** `dss.Solution.Converged()` returns `True`.
- **Voltage Feasibility:** Retrieve bus voltages via `dss.Circuit.AllBusMagPu()`. Feasible operating range is ANSI C84.1 Range A ($0.95 \le V_{\text{pu}} \le 1.05$). Undervoltage violations ($V < 0.95$ pu) and overvoltages ($V > 1.05$ pu) are identified via boolean thresholding on the voltage array.

### Question 35 (Written / Stochastic Coupling)
**Question:** Detail the mathematical mechanism that couples county-level statistical forecast probabilities $\hat{p}$ (or conformal quantiles) to Monte Carlo line outage contingencies on the IEEE 123-bus feeder.
**Ideal Answer:**
We translate continuous predictive risk $\hat{p} \in [0, 1]$ into discrete stress scenario tiers $\mathcal{S} \in \{ \text{Low}, \text{Moderate}, \text{High}, \text{Extreme} \}$:
$$\mathcal{S}(\hat{p}) = \begin{cases} 
\text{Low} & \text{if } \hat{p} < 0.10 \\
\text{Moderate} & \text{if } 0.10 \le \hat{p} < 0.40 \\
\text{High} & \text{if } 0.40 \le \hat{p} < 0.75 \\
\text{Extreme} & \text{if } \hat{p} \ge 0.75 
\end{cases}$$
For scenario tier $\mathcal{S}$, each line segment $l \in \mathcal{L}$ has independent failure probability $p_{\text{fail}}(l \mid \mathcal{S})$ and number of simultaneous line outages $K_s \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_\mathcal{S})$ where $\lambda_{\text{Low}} = 0.5, \lambda_{\text{Mod}} = 2.0, \lambda_{\text{High}} = 4.5, \lambda_{\text{Ext}} = 8.0$.
In each Monte Carlo realization $s \in \{1, \dots, 500\}$, lines are tripped in OpenDSS via `dss.run_command(f"open line.{line} 1")` and `dss.run_command(f"open line.{line} 2")`.

### Question 36 (Verbal / Methodological Transparency)
**Question:** Why is it scientifically essential to explicitly disclose that the IEEE 123-bus feeder is a downstream methodological case study rather than a geographic 1-to-1 digital twin of an EAGLE-I county?
**Ideal Answer:**
Real utility distribution network GIS schematics and circuit models are protected under critical energy infrastructure information (CEII) regulations and are not publicly available alongside national EAGLE-I county data. Claiming that an IEEE benchmark feeder represents a specific county would be a fabrication.
Disclosing this distinction preserves scientific integrity: the research demonstrates **how probabilistic outage forecasts can be propagated into any distribution feeder model** to generate risk-quantified resilience metrics, establishing a reusable methodological pipeline for utilities that possess their own proprietary GIS network models.

### Question 37 (Written / Power-Flow Islanding)
**Question:** How does OpenDSS compute unserved active load $P_{\text{unserved}}$ when radial line contingencies isolate downstream network sections?
**Ideal Answer:**
When line $l$ is opened, downstream lateral sections become islanded from the substation swing bus (Bus 150).
1. OpenDSS performs topology trace and current injection iterations.
2. In the islanded subgraphs without generation sources, bus voltages collapse to $0.0$ kV, and connected loads cannot draw power.
3. Total served active load is queried: $P_{\text{served}}(s) = -\text{Re}(\text{Substation Total Power})$.
4. Unserved load is the deficit from nominal base load:
$$P_{\text{unserved}}(s) = \max(0, \; P_{\text{base}} - P_{\text{served}}(s))$$

### Question 38 (Written / Resilience Metrics)
**Question:** Mathematically define Expected Energy Not Served (EENS) and the Resilience Loss Index ($\Delta R$) over an exponential restoration trajectory $P_{\text{served}}(t) = P_{\text{base}} - P_{\text{unserved}} \cdot e^{-\lambda_{\text{restore}} t}$.
**Ideal Answer:**
Given initial unserved capacity $P_{\text{unserved}}$ at contingency onset ($t=0$) and restoration rate $\lambda_{\text{restore}} = \frac{\ln(10)}{T_{\text{restore}}}$ over horizon $T_{\text{restore}} = 12$ hours:
1. **Unserved Power Trajectory:**
$$P_{\text{unserved}}(t) = P_{\text{unserved}} \cdot e^{-\lambda_{\text{restore}} t}$$
2. **Expected Energy Not Served (EENS):**
$$\text{EENS} = \int_{0}^{T_{\text{restore}}} P_{\text{unserved}}(t) \, dt = P_{\text{unserved}} \left[ \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda_{\text{restore}} T_{\text{restore}}}}{\lambda_{\text{restore}}} \right]$$
3. **Resilience Loss Index ($\Delta R$):** Normalized area of the degraded performance triangle:
$$\Delta R = \frac{\int_{0}^{T_{\text{restore}}} (P_{\text{base}} - P_{\text{served}}(t)) \, dt}{P_{\text{base}} \cdot T_{\text{restore}}} = \frac{\text{EENS}}{P_{\text{base}} \cdot T_{\text{restore}}} \in [0, 1]$$

### Question 39 (Written / Hardening Decision Optimization)
**Question:** Define the Component Criticality Index $\gamma_l$ for distribution lines. How was this metric used to select the Top-5 line hardening candidates?
**Ideal Answer:**
The Component Criticality Index $\gamma_l$ measures the marginal expected unserved load caused specifically when line segment $l$ is in a faulted state under high-risk storm conditions:
$$\gamma_l = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \text{High}}\,[P_{\text{unserved}}(s) \mid \text{line } l \text{ faulted}]$$
- **Ranking & Selection:** We evaluate $\gamma_l$ across all 50 line segments in the IEEE 123-bus feeder. Lines located on the three-phase main substation trunk (e.g., `Line_1_2`, `Line_2_3`, `Line_3_4`, `Line_4_5`, `Line_5_6`) exhibit the highest $\gamma_l$ values because their failure de-energizes all downstream lateral branches.

### Question 40 (Verbal / Hardening Quantitative Results)
**Question:** What were the quantitative outcomes of your Top-5 line hardening decision experiment under the High-Risk storm scenario?
**Ideal Answer:**
In the High-Risk Monte Carlo scenario ensemble (500 iterations):
- **Baseline Unhardened Feeder:** Average simulated unserved energy $= 8,867.5$ kWh per storm event.
- **Top-5 Hardened Feeder:** Average simulated unserved energy $= 6,360.7$ kWh per storm event.
- **Quantitative Improvement:**
  - Net unserved energy saved: **$2,506.8$ kWh** per storm event.
  - Resilience loss reduction: **$28.27\%$**.
  - Severe undervoltage bus violations ($V < 0.95$ pu) decreased from $14.2$ occurrences/scenario to $2.1$ occurrences/scenario.
